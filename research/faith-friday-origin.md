# Faith Friday origin note

`https://hitchhikertothefuture.substack.com/p/faith-friday` — Leo Guinan, published
2025-09-19. This post is the **origin document** for several concepts that later
became formal thesis elements. It is a first-person reflective essay, not a
measured claims ledger; the 87% figure in it is memetic, not calculated.

## Concepts this post originates or states

1. **Prediction methodology.** "I make specific predictions about outcomes
   because whether or not my predictions are right help me figure out whether or
   not I'm still on the right track. The more I can predict, the better I know
   I'm doing." This is the founding statement of the newsbin → claims-ledger
   falsification discipline: publish specific predictions, check them, publish
   the miss with the same weight as the hit.

2. **Shorting complexity.** "It's all going to get a lot dumber as people add AI.
   So I'm shorting complexity." The thesis that complexity/inefficiency rises as
   AI is added. Formalized as CLAIM-004, informed by the AcadeResearch 2026
   cohort (AI-adopter overhead step-up: Uber G&A+R&D 13.94% of revenue, Meta R&D
   35.6%).

3. **Time Violence.** Stated as: "How much time should something take to
   complete? How much time does it actually take to complete?" This is the same
   concept already formalized in the Proximity to the Future Fund workspace.

4. **Verification fog.** "Proof doesn't matter if the people you need to prove
   something to simply don't have the ability to understand it." The asymmetry
   between practitioners (who see actual knowhow) and the market (which cannot
   verify without practitioner experience) — the HumanPower Index lens.

## The 87% claim — memetic, not calculated

The post contains "I can predict market collapse with 87% accuracy but I can't
predict which LLC owns my domain names." Per Leo's instruction this 87% number
is memetic (for rhetorical effect), not a measured accuracy. It must NOT be
treated as a calibration figure. The real, falsifiable calibration claim is
CLAIM-005: directional predictions correct >50% over a declared sample.
